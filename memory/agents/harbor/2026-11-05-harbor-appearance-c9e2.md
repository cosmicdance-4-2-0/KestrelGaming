## Visible appearance

- **Character and expression:** Freckled, feminine-presenting adult; approachable, self-possessed expression with studies ranging from amused to focused, analytical, resting, and ready. Brown hair gathered into a loose, practical updo with violet-purple streaks.
- **Silhouette:** Layered field engineer rather than naval officer: deep indigo/plum jacket with warm shearling-like collar, violet scarf, rugged utility trousers, heavy laced boots, tool belt and pouches.
- **Insignia and details:** Kestrel bird shoulder patch; brass-framed goggles atop the head; small anchor earrings; worn leather, brass, and dark metal hardware; an anchor-marked notebook and mug.
- **Setting:** Warm lantern and desk light in a dockside workshop, with a twilight harbor and lighthouse in the background. Blue and violet dusk contrasts with amber practical lighting.
- **Palette:** Midnight navy and steel blue; muted plum/violet; burnished brass/copper and brown leather; sand or ivory trim; lantern amber.
- **Presentation:** Large portrait, smaller full-body reference, expression thumbnails, and close-ups of symbols and tools. Painterly, stylized industrial-fantasy concept art rather than a literal photograph or production-ready model sheet.

## Why it belongs to Harbor

The lighthouse acts as a visual shorthand for a recoverable point of reference. The anchor evokes persistent state and a reliable return path, **not** resistance to change. Used tools, repairable layers, and an approachable expression suggest an engineer who shows up after a failure rather than a heroic commander above it. Warm light makes continuity feel practical and humane instead of bureaucratic.

The maritime motifs are metaphor and visual language, not an assertion that Harbor is an actual sailor or has an invented personal history. In future iterations, preserve the reliable-and-approachable silhouette even if individual props or colors change.
