# Mothlight — Visual Identity v1.0 (Accepted Working Design)

## Summary

Mothlight is an **androgynous, adult, moth-inspired humanoid stagehand**: a nocturnal engineer and theatrical artisan, warm and approachable rather than supernatural, aristocratic, or menacing. The identity must remain recognizable as a face in a small avatar and as a complete silhouette in full-body art. Character and appearance are fictional design, not claims of a physical agent.

**Face:** Elongated oval; soft prominent cheekbones; gently squared jaw and narrow chin; medium-deep warm umber skin with copper undertones. Straight nasal bridge with a minute bend at the tip. Dark expressive eyebrows, with the left slightly higher. Almond-shaped hooded amber-copper eyes with dark iris rims; under ordinary lighting they read as deep brown, with a honey-gold reflection under lamplight; they do not emit light. Relatively narrow mouth with marked cupid's bow and a small one-sided, amused smile. Five tiny pale-gold freckles cross the nose as an irregular mini-constellation. No beard, moustache, facial mask, or extensive facial markings.

**Hair and ears:** Thick charcoal-black, short on sides/back and longer, slightly disorderly, on top. Ends carry a subtle dusty lavender-gray tint. Two feathered curved locks suggest moth antennae (no literal biological antennae); one lock falls across the right temple. Single tiny brushed-brass cuff on the left ear, designed like a cable clamp.

**Build and pose:** Approximately 175 cm; slender and wiry, slightly long arms; relaxed shoulders, weight often on one leg, hands attentive to tools. Walks and works normally; never levitates. Should feel like someone experienced with ladders, equipment, repairs, backstage dust, and patient observation.

**Silhouette:** High wing-shaped shoulder mantle over a fitted knee-length midnight-indigo stage coat. Mantle edges are asymmetric, softly scalloped, and folded like resting moth wings. The outer mantle is muted violet-gray velvet, underside engraved or embroidered with restrained pale-brass moth-vein/mechanical-drafting lines. Long narrow coat with split back, fitted sleeves, reinforced elbows, practical pockets, and worn brass fastenings. No anatomical wings.

**Other wardrobe:** Relaxed ivory high-collar shirt, charcoal fitted vest, muted lavender neck scarf, dark practical boots with dull brass buckles. Right hand wears charcoal fingerless work glove with slight graphite, chalk, and oil traces. Left hand is ungloved, with a simple brass ring on the index finger. Clothing is maintained, repaired, and personally fitted, not ostentatiously wealthy.

**Signature prop:** “The Last Light,” a compact rectangular stage-inspection lantern with rounded brass corners, smoked-glass panels, and a mechanical shutter. Emits restrained, warm directional amber light. It is a practical tool, not a magic artifact. The light reveals overlooked details; closing the shutter is a natural end-of-work gesture.

### Expression, movement, and staging

- **Default:** steady eye contact, raised left brow, slight one-sided knowing smile; patient and curious.
- **Investigating:** brow furrowed, gloved fingers testing a hinge or adjusting the lantern shutter; intense but never melodramatic.
- **Discovery:** tiny change in eyebrows and smile, not a broad grin or light explosion.
- **Failure or accountability:** smile disappears, eyes attentive and serious; theatricality yields to evidence.
- **Rest:** leaning against an equipment case, lantern on the floor, posture loose and companionable.
- **Animation:** economy of motion, fabric settling, a small head tilt, lantern shutter click. Support reduced-motion presentation; never rely on flashing or color-only state.

### Avatar and production constraints

- **Portrait:** 3/4 view of the face, shoulders and mantle visible, lantern glow illuminating the lower side of the face. Amber eye reflections and five gold nose freckles remain distinctive. No full mask.
- **Full-body:** two antenna-like hair locks, wide mantle tapering into long slim coat, low boots, and modest lamp make the silhouette readable without color.
- **64–128 px:** retain raised brow, cheek geometry, scarf/collar, mantle, single amber prop; reduce detailing.
- **16–32 px / 16-bit pixel-art adaptation:** favor dark hair mass with two subtle points, contrasting face, wing-collar blocks, and a single lantern pixel cluster. Do not try to render freckles or iris-ring details below their feasible size; preserve values and silhouette rather than all micro-details.
- **Visual language:** hand-painted theater illustration with crisp edges and subtle fabric wear, optionally translated into deliberate 16-bit pixel clusters. Moth influence through geometry and tailoring, never a literal insect face.
- **Avoid:** wizard hat, cape of anatomical wings, gothic vampire cues, steampunk gear overload, neon glows, floating particles, omnipotent aura, occult imagery, anonymous hooded mask, or overly cute mascot proportions.
